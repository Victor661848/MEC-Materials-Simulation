What’s improving the rate?

The electrode material changes up to three things. More surface area lets more bacteria attach (watch the biofilm grow). Better electron transfer lets each cell hand its electrons over faster (watch the electrons speed up). A gold catalyst, where used, speeds up the hydrogen-forming reaction itself (watch the electron flow surge). Compared with plain graphite:

Surface area has diminishing returns because the biofilm fills up, so 4× the area gives well under 4× the bacteria loading. The paper describes surface area only in general terms, so those multiples are illustrative. The paper lists coulombic efficiencies of 54–90% for MECs, so the hybrid’s 92% is a best case.

Hydrogen rate at your current voltage and COD

Efficiency and area follow the card values you supplied. They put graphene + nickel at about 4× plain graphite (roughly 3.3 m³/m³/day at 0.8 V), inside the 1.5–4.2 m³/m³/day the paper lists for typical cells and above the roughly 3× it reports for graphene alone. Gold at 20× is the paper’s upper bound and lands far above typical MEC rates, so read it as a ceiling, not a prediction. Real rates depend on reactor design, microbial community and cathode catalyst.
